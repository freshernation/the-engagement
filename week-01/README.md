# Week 1: Before the build

> **Destination**
> By Friday the customer's decision maker can read one page and say yes, no, or "yes
> if", and every sentence on that page traces back to something the customer told
> you.

**Project 1.** No code this week. Five documents, each built on the one before, and a
proposal defended in front of the person who pays.

---

## The week

| Day | Folder | What you'll be able to do |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | `day-1/` | Run a discovery interview that gets facts, not compliments |
| Tue | `day-2/` | Write the problem underneath the ask, with its number and its non-goals |
| Wed | `day-3/` | Turn the problem into requirements a customer can sign and an engineer can test |
| Thu | `day-4/` | Choose between two designs on paper, and estimate one honestly |
| Fri | `milestone/` | **Project 1**: the proposal, defended |

---

## The rule this week

**Nothing goes in a document that is not in your notes.** If you need a fact you do not
have, you go back and ask. A follow-up question costs the customer five minutes. A
proposal built on a guess costs the engagement.

---

## Monday morning

Read your client's `BRIEF.md` twice. Then read `day-1/README.md` before you write a
single question. Most people's first draft of questions is a list of ways to ask "would
you like what I am planning to build?", and the customer will say yes to all of them.

---

## Outside-view question

> *A good doctor does not ask a patient "would you like antibiotics?" They ask when it
> started, what it felt like, and what the patient has already tried. What would a
> doctor's first five questions be to your customer?*
