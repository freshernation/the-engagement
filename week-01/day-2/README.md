## What you need to know

### The ask is not the problem

Customers arrive with a solution: "a chatbot", "a summariser", "an agent that approves".
It is their best guess at a fix for something that hurts. Your job today is to write
down the hurt. Sometimes the solution they asked for is right. Usually it is a
neighbour of the right one. Occasionally it is the thing that must not be built.

### The number

A problem statement without a number is a feeling. You need three:

- **What it costs today**, in time, money or customers, from your notes.
- **The baseline**: where the measure is now.
- **The target**: where it must be, and by when, for the customer to call it fixed.

If you do not have them, today is a follow-up day. Draft the question first; ask it
about a specific recent event.

### Non-goals

The things that could reasonably be in scope and are not. Writing them down is the
cheapest protection an engagement has. Every non-goal you write today is an argument
you will not have in week 2.

### Why now

Something changed. A complaint, a competitor, a new boss, a lost customer. It is usually
the real reason the decision maker cares, and it decides what "done" means to them.
