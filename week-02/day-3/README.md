# Day 3: The scope change

> **By the end of today** you have answered an urgent request from the customer's boss
> with what it would cost, three ways to respond, a recommendation, and a reply they can
> forward upstairs.

---

## This morning

Open your customer chat and say: *"It's week 2, Wednesday."* The customer has something
to ask you.

---

## What you need to know

### Every engagement gets one

Somewhere in the middle, someone senior sees something, reads something, or loses
something, and the scope moves. It is not a failure. Handling it is the job. The two ways
to get it wrong are opposite: saying yes to everything, and saying no to everything.

### The impact, in their units

Days, rupees, and what happens to the target. "That's a lot of work" is not an impact.
"It adds 8 to 12 days, the date moves from the 24th to early next month, and the summary
target slips because the same two people would build both" is.

### Three options

1. **Do it**, and what moves to make room.
2. **Swap it**: do it instead of something on the current list, and name what.
3. **Defer it**: finish what was agreed, then scope this properly, with a date for that
   conversation.

Sometimes there is a fourth: a much smaller version that answers the worry behind the
request. Look for it. It is often the one they take.

### The reply

The customer will forward it. Under 150 words, answer first, no jargon, and a decision
they can make today.
