# Sources

Short on purpose. Each one is here because a specific day uses it.

| Day | Source | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 Mon | Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test* (2013). [momtestbook.com](https://www.momtestbook.com/) | The best short book on asking questions that people cannot answer politely. Read chapters 1 and 2 |
| Week 1 Mon | Palantir, [*Dev versus Delta: Demystifying engineering roles at Palantir*](https://blog.palantir.com/dev-versus-delta-demystifying-engineering-roles-at-palantir-ad44c2a6e87) | The company that named the role, on what a forward deployed engineer is judged by: impact on one customer's goal |
| Week 1 Tue | Colin Bryar and Bill Carr, *Working Backwards* (2021), the chapter on the press release and FAQ | Writing the outcome down before you build, in the customer's words |
| Week 1 Thu | Malte Ubl, [*Design Docs at Google*](https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/) (2020) | Context, goals and non-goals, the design, alternatives considered, cross-cutting concerns. `DESIGN.md` follows it |
| Week 1 Thu | Steve McConnell, *Software Estimation: Demystifying the Black Art* (2006), chapter 1 | An estimate is not a target and not a commitment. Read the first chapter only |
| Week 1 Fri | Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle* | Answer first, then the reasons. How to write for someone who reads one paragraph |
| Week 2 Thu | Your own `RUNBOOK.md` and `ARCHITECTURE.md` from Agent In Production | You have written a handover before. This time it is for a customer |

## What to ignore

Consulting frameworks with acronyms. Templates that are mostly headings. Anything
that tells you to "align stakeholders" without telling you what to write down.
